# Workbench

The Workbench is the default place to **make and arrange** a piece. Its ordered cards are sections of your draft. Select a card in **Comfortable** density and click **Writing · reader-facing prose** to edit the real section text. **Storyboard · private note / AI context** is a separate note about what that part should do; it is not printed as prose. **Overview** is for scanning the arrangement. **Edit in preview** puts the same editor back in the assembled page.

At the top, **New thought** captures a Freeform card into Draft or Parked. Enter adds it; Shift+Enter makes a line. The first draft thought uses an untouched empty starter section. Use **Add draft section**, **+ Add before**, **+ Add after**, or a boundary **+** to choose a type and place. Drag cards to reorder within their area, or use section options to move them. Card names and semantic roles are independent of the writing. A Segue or Transition can show the previous and next sections as **read-only** context; select a neighbor separately to edit it.

**Parked Thoughts** sits below the reader draft. A parked card is still editable and can go into an Ungrouped area or a named collapsible group. **Read parked thought in Preview** opens that one thought wide; it does not add it to the draft. **Include in draft** asks for a position before a draft section or at the end. [Writing a piece](writing-a-piece.md) explains the section boundary.

**Layout** presets (Writing, Review, Workbench only, All panes, Reset layout) and Show/Hide controls change what you see, not what you wrote. You can resize panes; **Focus preview** temporarily widens the assembled reading area, and **Restore panes** returns to your arrangement. The Inspector holds the [Lab](#ask-about-one-target) and optional tools. On a narrow window the panes stack, so you may need to scroll.

## Ask about one target

Choose a word, passage or section, then check the target shown in the Inspector. Give **Your direction**, run a diagnosis and, for creative proposals, answer the question in your own words before requesting options. The tools can read broader context but a replacement applies only to the selected target in its section. Whole-piece critique is analysis, not a whole-document replacement. Results and your drafts stay with their section when you switch away.

You can inspect or edit a candidate, compare models or save a [Take](takes.md) without changing the draft. Only an explicit **Accept / Replace / Use this version** changes prose; **Reject** leaves it alone. A changed target may make an older candidate unusable rather than applying it to similar words. [Models and routing](models-and-routing.md) covers optional model choices; [Technical Writing](technical-writing.md) and [Word/Phrase and Delivery tools](#words-phrases-and-delivery) cover narrower questions.

## Words, phrases and Delivery

Selecting a word or short phrase (up to eight words, without sentence-ending punctuation) opens the Word/Phrase Lens. **Explore** explains distinctions; **Replace** previews possible alternatives within the surrounding sentence. Exact, balanced and loose fidelity are requests, not promises of identical meaning. **Delivery** examines how punctuation, spacing, casing or a short passage may land. It is analysis only, with no replacement button. Offline lexical entries and Delivery rules are limited; they do not know current usage or your full intent.

Use **Find a tool** or `Cmd/Ctrl+K` if a control is hard to find. It routes you to the existing surface; typing `/` in the draft is just writing, not a command.

## Structure without a template

**Structure · assemble a thought** helps you consider how two thoughts connect. Enter your own A and B (or segment rough notes, then choose what belongs in each slot), pick a relationship and a scaffold, and inspect the preview. Connectors are explanations and options, not automatic replacements. The builder substitutes your words literally, so check punctuation and casing yourself. **Stage for target** saves a human-built proposal to inspect; only explicit acceptance edits the draft. An analysis or critique of the relationship does not apply prose. [Personal Library](personal-library.md) can keep a pattern or move for another piece.
