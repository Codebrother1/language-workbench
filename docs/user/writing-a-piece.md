# Writing a piece

A piece is an ordered set of **sections**. Each section holds your prose, an optional short label and a **semantic type** describing its job. You can repeat a type, skip one or change it later. A Hook, Point or Segue is not a compulsory slot in a template; Freeform is fine when you do not yet know a part's job.

Write in the page, or capture rough thoughts in **New thought** and arrange them later. **Add draft section** and the **+** at a section boundary insert at a chosen place; the type picker also offers **All section types**. Section options let you insert, duplicate, change type, move, split, merge or delete parts. A short label helps you find a part without changing its prose or role. Deleting the final section leaves an empty Freeform place to write.

**Park thought** is for something that belongs to the project but not today's reading order, perhaps an opening you may use later. Parked sections keep their writing, notes, takes and identity. They remain editable in the Workbench, can be grouped, and are excluded from Document View, reader word count, **Copy whole piece** and reading downloads. **Include in draft** makes you choose a draft position. Parked material remains in document JSON for recovery. See [Workbench](workbench.md).

Native typing, selection, paste, lists and headings work inside a section. Select one section's prose to revise it. To change the structure, use section commands: ordinary typing, deletion, cut or paste **across section boundaries is blocked** so a casual edit cannot join or erase section identities. Copy across sections is allowed.

Use [Sources](sources.md) for material you are consulting, [Writing Brief](writing-brief.md) for your intentions and [Piece Memory](piece-memory.md) for where you left off. None of those is a second draft. When the order feels right, read it in [Document View](document-view.md).
